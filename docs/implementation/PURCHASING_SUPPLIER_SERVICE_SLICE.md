# First supplier-service purchasing contract

**Product:** v0.0.1. **Contract:** owner-accepted on 2026-10-10 through [COM-016's proposal](../review/COM_016_DECISION_PROPOSAL.md). **Implementation state:** locally `PRODUCTION_HONEST`, `BLOCKED = none` for this selected scope; exact source, evidence and recovery are recorded in [the qualification receipt](../review/COM_016_OPS_013_IMPLEMENTATION_RECEIPT.md).

## Selected case and ownership

Purchasing owns one tenant's informal supplier request for a single non-stock service, such as printing, with optional attribution to exactly one line of a committed tenant Order. Supplier identity/contact is a purchasing-owned business role, not the purged Party model. Catalog owns the selected unit; Orders owns linked committed facts. The purchase retains its unit identity/code/precision and any Order/line/revision reference. Supplier costs never change selling prices, create customer receivables, settle customer balances, or create stock or fulfillment effects.

Use a required supplier name up to 128 Unicode scalar values, optional contact text up to 512, service description up to 512, optional payment reference up to 128, and required correction/cancellation reasons up to 512. Reject malformed UTF-16 and controls before outer trimming. Unit precision is the existing Catalog's 0–4. Quantities are positive and exact at that precision; no rounding, tolerance or conversion is introduced. NPR receipt costs, payments and refunds are positive exact two-decimal amounts bounded by decimal(19,2); receipt cost is the operator-recorded total for that receipt. Queries are tenant-scoped and read-only, with at most 50 list items and 10 history items per page; command bodies are bounded at 8 KiB. Each purchase retains at most 128 financial entries, including voided entries, and a current or receipt snapshot of at most 64 KiB in both canonical application JSON and PostgreSQL stored `jsonb::text` representation. Further recording fails explicitly without partial effect when a limit is reached; corrections preserve original journal facts. Queries and historical reads reject unsupported or malformed retained data rather than truncating it.

## Supplier, request and financial facts

Supplier IDs and historical snapshots are stable. Management can create/revise/retire identity/contact under expected revision; retirement prevents new requests and preserves existing receipt/payment history. Requests record one supplier, description, requested quantity/unit and server UTC actor/time facts. Informal phone requests are valid without a formal purchase order or linked Order.

Each actual partial receipt records its quantity and actual NPR total cost, creating an equal payable. Cumulative effective receipt quantity cannot exceed the request. Received facts and costs are immutable journal facts; current projections do not silently rewrite them. Cancellation records only the unreceived remainder, prevents further receipt recording, and preserves received/payable facts. It does not prove supplier-side contractual cancellation or reverse an actual received service.

Cash/bank supplier payments allocate positive amounts to exactly one receipt payable. Effective payments cannot exceed its current cost. A separately recorded actual supplier refund links to its payment/payable history and cannot exceed the refundable paid amount. Outstanding balance is current cost minus effective payments plus actual refunds, independently of customer balances. Concurrent/retried receipt/payment/refund/correction commands cannot over-receive or over-allocate.

Corrections require a current initial Owner, the distinct correction right, recent trusted Tenant Web strong authentication under the existing admission policy, expected revision, target fact and reason. They append a linked reversal/replacement while retaining originals. Corrected receipt quantity stays within the request; payable cost cannot fall below effective payments until an actual supplier refund covers the difference. Dependent refund/payment links must remain valid. Corrections do not infer a physical return, cash transfer, customer remedy, tax treatment or general-ledger effect.

## Authority, durability and boundary direction

Independent rights are `purchasing.supplier.view`, `purchasing.supplier.manage`, `purchasing.request.record`, `purchasing.receipt.record`, `purchasing.payment.record`, and `purchasing.correct`. No right implies another or is granted automatically by an Owner title. All protected reads, commands and replays require current active membership and the applicable pinned-model permission. Source lookup uses owning public Catalog/Orders boundaries; Purchasing does not query their private tables or expose their financial facts.

PostgreSQL owns durable supplier/request/projection/journal/command-receipt facts with forced tenant RLS, scoped keys, explicit expected revisions and caller/operation-scoped semantic idempotency. Authoritative effect, attributable journal and receipt commit atomically. Replay returns retained original results under fresh authority; changed intent conflicts. Runtime identities cannot rewrite/delete retained journals and receipts. Queries cannot hide mutations. Unsupported/malformed retained versions fail closed; errors and diagnostics exclude contacts, descriptions, amounts, references, reasons, credentials and provider payloads.

CoreApi and DbMigrator compose the capability and its PostgreSQL adapter. New physical projects are earned only for provider-neutral business rules, provider-isolated durability, and the pure/native tests proving those boundaries. No feature catalog/profile expansion, broker, background Worker, supplier API, generic repository, audit service or procurement framework is selected.

## Receiving API and recovery contract

CoreApi exposes tenant-relative `/api/v1/tenants/{tenantId}/purchasing`. Supplier commands use `/suppliers/{supplierId}` (create), `/revise`, and `/retire`; request commands use `/requests/{purchaseId}` (create), `/receipts`, `/payments`, `/refunds`, `/corrections`, and `/cancel`. The caller supplies a stable non-empty resource GUID and one printable ASCII `Idempotency-Key` of at most 128 characters. Every command supplies `expectedRevision`: zero for creation, positive for a later command. Changed intent under the same caller/operation/key conflicts across resource IDs. Current authority is required even when returning a retained original receipt after restart.

Requests carry `supplierId`, `unitId`, `description`, `quantity`, and optional `order` with `orderId`, `linePosition`, `orderRevision`. Receipt cost is `cost`; payment/refund amount is `amount`, with exact method values `Cash` or `Bank` and optional `reference`. Payment uses `receiptId`; refund uses `paymentId`. Correction uses `targetEventId`, `reason`, and an explicitly supplied nullable `replacement`; null voids the erroneous record, while replacement retains its financial kind and allocation links. Correcting an erroneous allocation requires correcting/voiding its mistaken dependent records before recording the valid allocation. Cancellation stays closed after later corrections; it does not silently reopen supplier work. The current projection exposes any resulting unfulfilled quantity separately.

Command responses return only `id` and `revision`, with `Idempotency-Replayed: true` on replay. They grant no broad supplier/history/cost read access. The separate viewer right protects supplier and request detail, lists, histories, and `/suppliers/{supplierId}/balance`. Lists accept `limit` (1–50) and versioned tenant/collection-bound `after`; histories accept `limit` (1–10) and `beforeRevision`. History follows retained revision keysets; no across-request database snapshot is claimed. Balance streams validated bounded purchase snapshots with constant application memory, checked decimal arithmetic and caller cancellation. No durable read-side mutation is performed.

Transport rejects duplicate, unknown and missing required JSON members, invalid enum spellings, oversized bodies and malformed queries. Purchasing owns decimal, dependency, revision, source and correction invariants. Native PostgreSQL uses immutable source/events/receipts, projection-column-only updates, caller/operation-key and aggregate locks, expected revisions, forced RLS and deferred evidence completeness guards. Reads and replays compare typed head, source, journal and receipt contracts; malformed/unsupported evidence fails closed with safe errors. No raw provider exception, financial text or request body is a public error contract.

Fresh command authority and Catalog/Orders source checks after lock acquisition reuse the held connection and transaction through provider-owned public query ports. They do not lease a second connection from the same pool. Tenancy owns its active account/tenant/membership, authorization revision and initial-Owner observation; Catalog owns the unit query and publication pin; Orders owns a single-statement committed-line reference query that exposes no customer or selling-price data. A before/after authority observation change fails closed. The native CoreApi journey must use the configured production data-source factory at pool sizes one and two, including bounded concurrent commands; an unrestricted test pool is insufficient evidence for this guarantee.
